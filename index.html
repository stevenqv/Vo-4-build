About

Welcome to Onion Sketchcraft, a website dedicated to teaching upcoming artists on how to improve their drawing skills by following the guide listed below! The first step you need to do is to learn the fundamental portions of drawing before moving on to using them to build up figure sketches and backgrounds. Once you mastered both of them, you are now able to create simple clean and appealing animations, minding their key principles and features.

Fundamentals of Drawing

Every artist need to start somewhere, so the first thing you need to do is to understand the key principles of what makes a drawing excellent. Here are some you need to know:

Lines

Lines are the backbone of every drawings ever, even lineless ones. Some of the most recognizable lines are:

  • Straight Lines
  • Wavy Lines
  • Curved Lines
  • Dotted/Dashed Lines
  • Zigzagged Lines

Shapes

After the lines are intersected, shapes are created. They are essential for building objects and characters in figure sketches as well as backgrounds. They comes in two types:

  • 2D Shapes: Flat forms that does not have any depths shown (circles, squares, and triangles).
  • 3D Shapes: Forms with depth that are essential to constructing complex subjects like items and characters (spheres, cubes, and cylinders).

Values

Values not only define the light and dark areas of a drawing, but also what makes it three-dimensional in the first place.

  • Tint (lighter hue)
  • Tones (grey variant of hue)
  • Shade (darker hue)

Textures and Shading Techniques

Textures define how the drawing should feel. One way to express this was through shading techniques, particularly when using only one color. Here are some common techniques to consider:

  • Hatching: Single directional lines
  • Cross-hatching: Multiple and intersecting directional lines for rough effects
  • Stippling: Little dots on varying spaces
  • Blending: Smooth shading through smudging lines to emulate a smoother feel
  • Scribbling: Random yet mostly continuous line to portray an energetic feel

Color Theory

Before you start coloring, please understand what a color theory is, including color harmonies, to make the drawing look more appealing to the viewers.

  • Primary Colors: Red, Yellow, and Blue
  • Secondary Colors: Green, Orange, and Purple

Notable Color Harmonies

  • Complementary Colors: Opposite hues on the color wheels
  • Analogues Colors: Neighboring colors on the color wheel
  • Monochromatic Color: Single color with varying values
7 Principles of Drawing

Finally, with the essential knowledge of what makes a drawing, now you need to understand how the drawing looks good according to its principles:

  • Proportions
  • Balance
  • Contrast
  • Emphasis
  • Unity
  • Rhythm
  • Movement
